# Editorial decisions

## Input and deliverables

Record a brief with audience, one main takeaway, duration range, narration language, aspect ratio, tone, reference preferences, available media, and production constraints. Missing optional preferences may be resolved from the channel profile; missing claims or recordings must not be fabricated.

Work through a beat map before assigning effects. Identify hook, explanation, evidence, qualification, and payoff. A scene's `takeaway` describes what the viewer should understand. Its `holdReason` describes why the timing serves that understanding. Each action's `reason` describes a meaningful change or focus shift.

Draft an animatic using real voiceover when available. Resolve structure and comprehension before polishing motion and sound. Do not accelerate all narration or collapse every pause to a fixed duration: compare cadence, breath and comprehension in the actual recording.

## Decisions by scene

| Scene | Prefer | Review for |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | One legible question or contrast; delayed payoff where appropriate | A promise the video actually fulfils |
| Mechanism | Establish subjects, show action, then consequence | Whether motion explains the mechanism rather than implying causation unsupported by the script |
| Comparison | Matched scale, alignment, labels and timing | Changes other than the variable being compared |
| Evidence | Readable finding, source and caveat when provided | A schematic or animation mistaken for empirical proof |
| Summary | Restate the resolved question in the same visual language | New unsupported information introduced at the end |

## Cases that require judgment

- Static diagrams may need several seconds of reading time. Flag the hold for review; do not repair it by adding a camera beat.
- An effect can be appropriate for one humorous reveal and excessive in a serious explanation. Preserve scene purpose when considering channel preferences.
- A cut can be cleaner than a transition. Select continuity, cut, or a motivated camera move; never add a generic whoosh by default.
- Keep caveats visible and timed with the claim they qualify. Do not let an emphatic headline or image contradict a careful narration.
- Reuse positions when continuing an explanation. If a visual metaphor changes, introduce the new relationship clearly.

## Worked examples

`professional-editor/examples/mechanism.json`: a small subject approaches a modular cell, then separates; the next scene inherits the exact previous state. No periodic pulse is added to the background.

`comparison.json`: equal panels and equal-sized subjects isolate a shape change. Neither a risk percentage nor a clinical benefit is invented.

`evidence.json`: a deliberate reading hold separates a schematic explanation from claims about effects in people. Its warning about a static hold is review information, not a failed edit.

These studies have no VO and remain drafts. For a narrated production, time them to the actual recording rather than adopting their durations unchanged.
