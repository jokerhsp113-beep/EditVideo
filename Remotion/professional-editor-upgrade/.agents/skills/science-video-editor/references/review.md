# Review and acceptance

## Technical blockers

Missing or rejected media, stale catalog hashes, missing/ambiguous anchors, invalid parent references, unknown layer/action kinds, unintended timeline gaps, out-of-range audio, and unsupported schemas block rendering through the validation CLI. Runtime validation also rejects malformed plans and unusable assets.

Final status additionally requires used assets to be approved with source and license metadata, no known placeholder audio, and completed visual/audio review. These are recorded decisions, not approvals a checker may invent.

## Review actual media

Render scene boundaries, action beginnings/midpoints/endings and the final frame. Inspect the primary subject, typography, alpha edges, attachments, captions and spatial continuity. Watch the segment as video for pacing, motion and flashes. A contact sheet samples frames; it cannot certify an entire timeline.

Listen to narration, music and SFX together where present. Technical audio analysis does not establish intelligibility or taste. Review the claim and caveat against the supplied script and sources; do not claim scientific verification based on an animation.

## Editorial rubric

| Dimension | Initial weight | Question |
|---|---:|---|
| Comprehension | 30 | Can the intended audience describe the takeaway correctly? |
| Timing | 20 | Can they follow actions and read information without dead repetition? |
| Visual consistency | 20 | Are hierarchy, scale, palette and alignment coherent? |
| Purposeful motion | 15 | Does each motion explain or direct attention? |
| Sound | 15 | Is voice clear and emphasis selective? |

Use an anchored 1..5 scale with written evidence. Do not compare totals when a dimension was not assessed; use a labelled visual-only score for silent studies. Self-assigned scores do not demonstrate five years of editing experience.

## Feedback record

Store issues with scene ID/timecode, severity, observed behavior, why it matters, proposed repair and verification. Record pending checks separately from passed checks. Preserve the user's actual preferences, but do not turn a scene-specific preference into a universal prohibition.

Evaluate substantial skill changes on new material, including missing assets, repeated anchors, long reading holds and unknown provenance. Compare with a baseline; avoid rewarding only resemblance to the examples. An audience A/B comparison should conceal which version is newer and ask comprehension questions as well as preference.
